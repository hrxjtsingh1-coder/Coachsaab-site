Why We Rewrote Our Core Engine in Rust

Jan 28, 2026 • Coach Saab • 10 min read

Python is the king of AI prototyping. It is easy to write, has massive libraries, and everyone knows it. But when you move to production—serving millions of requests per minute—Python becomes expensive.

Here is the story of why our team made the painful but necessary decision to migrate our core inference engine from Python to Rust, and why it saved us 60% on our cloud bill.

1. The Memory Safety Guarantee

In C++, you manage memory manually. One mistake, and you have a memory leak or a segfault. In Python, the Garbage Collector handles it, but at the cost of performance pauses.

Rust offers a third way: Ownership and Borrowing. The compiler ensures memory safety at compile time. This means zero runtime overhead for garbage collection and zero chance of null pointer exceptions. Our system stability went from 99.9% to 99.999% overnight.

2. Fearless Concurrency

AI workloads are heavily parallel. Python's Global Interpreter Lock (GIL) is a notorious bottleneck for multi-threaded applications. To get around it, you have to use complex multi-processing workarounds.

Rust allows for "Fearless Concurrency." We were able to saturate all 64 cores of our AWS Graviton instances without race conditions. The throughput increase was staggering.

3. The Financial Impact

This isn't just about code purity; it's about money. Because Rust is so efficient, we were able to downgrade our server fleet.

We moved from expensive `p4d.24xlarge` instances to smaller clusters, reducing our monthly AWS bill by nearly 60%. In this economy, efficiency is the best feature you can ship.
